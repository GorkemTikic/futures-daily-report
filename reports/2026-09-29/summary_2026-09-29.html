07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Bearish — Sentiment or outlook that expects prices to fall. A bearish trader or market believes prices are likely to go down.
Bullish — Sentiment or outlook that expects prices to rise. A bullish trader or market believes prices are likely to go up.
Collateral — Assets put up as security for a loan or credit facility. If the borrower defaults, the lender can take the collateral.
Funding rate — The cost traders pay to hold long positions in a futures contract. Expressed as a percentage per 8-hour period. Higher rates mean traders are more bullish; negative rates mean they're bearish and short-sellers are getting paid.
Leverage — Borrowing money to trade larger amounts than your own capital. A 2x leverage lets you trade with double your money but doubles both gains and losses.
Long — A bet that the price will go up. A trader holding a long position profits if the price rises.
Long/short ratio — The ratio of traders betting up (long) to traders betting down (short). A 1.5:1 ratio means 50% more traders are bullish than bearish.
Market breadth — The percentage of all coins trading on an exchange that went up on the day. 49% means about half went up and half went down — a neutral signal. Higher percentages (e.g., 70%) suggest broad bullish momentum.
Open interest — The total amount of money bet on price moves in futures contracts. Higher open interest means more traders are active in the market; rapid increases or decreases can signal shifting sentiment.
Short — A bet that the price will go down. A trader holding a short position profits if the price falls.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of aggressive buyers to aggressive sellers in a futures market. Above 1.0 means more aggressive buying; below 1.0 means more aggressive selling.
Top-trader long/short ratio — The ratio of long to short bets among the largest traders (usually the top 0.1% by account size). Shows what the biggest money is doing, separate from the average trader.
