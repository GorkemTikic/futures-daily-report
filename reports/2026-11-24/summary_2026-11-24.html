🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin and Ethereum each fell about 2% on November 24. Three main things happened: First, a newspaper said the US government might add taxes on goods from China and other countries soon, which scared people. Second, more people pulled money out of Bitcoin investment funds than ever before in a month. Third, when prices dropped, people who borrowed money to buy Bitcoin got forced to sell their coins automatically, which made prices fall faster. Almost no other cryptocurrencies went up—only 1 out of 6. It was a worrying day.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The price difference between a futures contract and the spot price. A positive basis means futures trade above spot; utc-day basis resets daily at 00:00 UTC.
Breadth — The percentage of coins in the market that advanced (went up) versus declined (went down) on a given day. Very low breadth (like 17.9%) signals a broad market decline, not selective weakness.
Funding rate — A periodic payment between traders holding long and short positions in perpetual futures. Positive rates mean long traders pay short traders (traders expect prices to rise). Rates are expressed as a raw percentage (e.g., 0.0001) per 8-hour period or as an annualized percentage.
Liquidation — Automatic forced closure of a leveraged trading position when collateral falls below a broker's threshold, triggering a sale at market prices. Liquidations in a falling market cause additional selling pressure.
Long/short ratio — The proportion of traders holding long (betting prices will rise) versus short (betting prices will fall) positions. A 2.5-to-1 ratio means 2.5 longs for every 1 short—very bullish positioning.
Mark price — The reference price used to calculate margin and trigger liquidations on perpetual futures, typically derived from multiple exchanges to prevent manipulation.
Open interest (OI) — The total number of open (unsettled) contracts or coins in perpetual futures markets at a point in time, valued in either coins or USD. Rising OI signals new money entering; falling OI signals liquidations or position closures.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of market buy orders filled (takers) to market sell orders filled. Above 1.0 means more aggressive buying; below 1.0 means more aggressive selling.
Top trader position ratio — The long-to-short ratio among the largest traders on an exchange. Often more balanced than the account-wide ratio because big money is typically less one-sided than retail.
Volume (USD) — The total notional value of contracts or coins traded (bought and sold) in a 24-hour period, measured in US dollars. High volume on a down day suggests forced selling; low volume suggests lack of conviction.
