🗿 The day, explained simply

Binance, the world's biggest place to buy and sell Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies, had a serious security problem: someone stole about $5.7 billion of customer money. When this news spread on Thursday, people got scared and sold their Bitcoin and Ethereum (the two biggest cryptocurrencies). When a lot of people sell at the same time, prices fall. Bitcoin went from $81,300 down to about $77,970, and Ethereum fell about 4.5%. Companies like Tether and Circle that issue special stable coins froze about $2.6 billion to protect it from theft. The US government also released interest-rate reports that day which made things worse. Most smaller cryptocurrencies fell even more than Bitcoin. This was a very worrying day—the worst kind of day for crypto traders.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The time period or measurement method used to report price changes and volume (e.g., 'utc-day' means a calendar day in UTC).
Breadth — The percentage of coins or assets in a market that moved up versus down. High breadth (many coins up) signals broad buying; low breadth (most coins down) signals broad selling.
Exchange — A platform where people trade cryptocurrencies for other cryptocurrencies or traditional money.
Funding rate — A small payment perpetual-futures traders make to each other every 8 hours. A positive rate means those betting prices will rise (longs) pay those betting prices will fall (shorts), and vice versa. Extreme rates signal overconfidence in one direction.
Hot wallet — An exchange's or service's internet-connected digital storage for user funds, used for withdrawals and deposits. Hot wallets are easier for hackers to access than offline cold storage.
Long / short (position) — A 'long' position is a bet that an asset's price will rise; a 'short' position is a bet that it will fall. A long/short ratio (e.g., '2-to-1 long') means twice as many traders are betting up as betting down.
Long/short ratio — The ratio of account-holders with long (bet price rises) versus short (bet price falls) positions. A ratio above 1 means more accounts are long; below 1 means more are short. Not the same as leverage—just direction bias.
Mark price — The reference price used to calculate profits and losses on perpetual futures. It is calculated from recent trades and indices to prevent manipulation.
Open interest — The total value or number of open positions in a perpetual futures market at a specific point in time. Rising open interest means more positions are being opened; falling means they are being closed.
Open-interest change — The percentage change in total open positions between two points in time. Rising OI into falling prices is often a warning sign of forced liquidations to come.
Perpetual futures (perps) — A type of leveraged contract that lets traders bet on an asset's price going up or down without a fixed expiration date. Unlike a futures contract with an end date, perpetuals can stay open indefinitely.
Stablecoin — A type of cryptocurrency designed to hold a fixed value (e.g., 1 USDC = $1 USD). Stablecoins are used as a safe way to move money between exchanges without price risk.
Taker buy/sell ratio — A measure of how aggressively buyers versus sellers are initiating trades during a period. A ratio above 1 means buyers are more aggressive; below 1 means sellers are more aggressive.
Top-trader long/short ratio — The long versus short position ratio among the largest traders on an exchange (not all traders). It shows which direction the biggest, smartest money is positioned.
Volume — The total value or number of trades in a market during a period, usually measured in 24 hours. High volume indicates active trading; low volume can mean illiquid or thin markets.
