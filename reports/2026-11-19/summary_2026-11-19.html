🗿 The day, explained simply

On Thursday, Bitcoin dropped 1.7% and Ethereum dropped 1.1% because almost everything was selling off that day. The U.S. stock market fell harder (Nasdaq down 2.2%), and when stocks drop, people often sell cryptocurrencies too. A big reason was that Anthropic, an AI company, started selling stock on the Nasdaq — it opened way above its asking price but then prices came back down, which made everyone nervous and kept selling. The market hit extreme fear levels (the scariest level ever recorded). Almost 8 out of every 10 cryptocurrencies lost money that day — it wasn't just Bitcoin, everything was down. This was definitely a very worrying day.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The price difference between a futures contract and the underlying spot price, used to measure whether futures are trading above (premium) or below (discount) the current market price
Breadth — The percentage or count of assets that moved up versus down on a given day; breadth below 50% (more down than up) signals weak or bearish market conditions
Death cross — A technical pattern where a shorter-term moving average (usually 50-day) crosses below a longer-term moving average (usually 200-day), often interpreted as a bearish signal
Funding rate — A periodic payment in perpetual futures markets where traders holding long positions pay traders holding short positions (or vice versa), designed to keep contract prices aligned with spot price
Long position — A bet that an asset's price will rise; the holder benefits if the price increases
Long/short ratio — The proportion of traders holding long positions (betting price will rise) compared to those holding short positions (betting price will fall); ratios above 1.0 mean more longs
Mark price — The reference price used in perpetual futures contracts to calculate profits and losses; designed to prevent manipulation by using a weighted average of spot and futures prices
Open interest — The total number of open (unsettled) positions in a futures market; rising OI shows growing leverage, falling OI shows positions being closed
Open interest change — The day-over-day increase or decrease in total open positions; positive change means more leverage entering the market, negative means leverage exiting
Perpetual futures — Derivative contracts that track a spot price with no expiration date, allowing traders to take leveraged long or short positions with 8-hour funding rate settlements
Short position — A bet that an asset's price will fall; the holder benefits if the price decreases
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of market buy orders to sell orders; above 1.0 indicates more aggressive buying, below 1.0 indicates more aggressive selling by takers (users accepting posted prices)
Top position ratio — The long-to-short ratio among the largest traders in the market; indicates whether the biggest accounts are more bullish or bearish
Volume — The total value or quantity of assets traded on an exchange over a specific period; higher volume usually indicates stronger conviction behind a price move
