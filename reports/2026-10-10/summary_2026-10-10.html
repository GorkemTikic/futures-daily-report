Bitcoin and Ethereum both went up a little bit on Saturday, October 10. Bitcoin is worth about $82,945 and Ethereum about $2,505. The exciting part is that most other crypto tokens also went up—about two-thirds of them—which means people were buying more than selling. 07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The difference between the price of a futures contract and the underlying spot price. Can indicate whether traders are bullish (positive basis) or bearish (negative basis).
Funding rate — The interest rate that long-position holders pay to short-position holders (or vice versa) in perpetual futures contracts. Positive rates mean longs are paying shorts; this happens when longs outnumber shorts and traders expect higher prices. Negative rates mean shorts are paying longs.
Liquidation — When a trader's losses in a leveraged position grow so large that the exchange automatically closes the position to prevent further losses. Can trigger a cascade of forced selling if many traders are liquidated at once.
Long/short ratio — The number of open long contracts divided by the number of open short contracts on a derivatives exchange. A ratio above 1.0 means more longs than shorts, suggesting traders expect prices to rise; below 1.0 means more shorts than longs.
Mark price — The reference price used by derivatives exchanges to calculate trader profits and losses on perpetual futures. Different from the last traded price and designed to prevent extreme swings due to low liquidity.
Market breadth — The percentage of trading pairs (or coins) that rose on a given day. Higher breadth (e.g., 66%) means most cryptocurrencies are up, suggesting broad-based strength; lower breadth means weakness is widespread.
Open interest — The total number of unfilled long and short contracts in derivatives markets. Growing open interest suggests new money entering; shrinking open interest suggests exits or liquidations.
Perpetual futures — Leveraged crypto derivatives contracts with no expiration date. Traders can hold them indefinitely as long as they maintain collateral, and funding rates keep long and short prices aligned with the spot market.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of aggressive buy orders to aggressive sell orders. Above 1.0 means more people are buying aggressively (pushing price up); below 1.0 means more aggressive selling (pushing price down).
