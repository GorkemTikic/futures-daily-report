07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

basis — The type of time period used for calculating daily returns: 'utc-day' means 00:00–23:59 UTC; 'rolling-24h' means the previous 24 hours from the snapshot time.
Fear & Greed Index — A 0–100 sentiment gauge measuring market mood: 0–20 = extreme fear, 20–40 = fear, 40–60 = neutral, 60–80 = greed, 80–100 = extreme greed. Based on volatility, momentum, social media, and Google search trends.
funding rate — A periodic payment between long and short traders on perpetual futures contracts. If positive, longs pay shorts (market tilted bullish). If negative, shorts pay longs (market tilted bearish). Expressed as an annualized percentage.
liquidation — Forced closure of a leveraged position when the margin (collateral) falls below the exchange's minimum. A trader's position is sold at market price, sometimes at a loss.
long / short position — Long: betting the price will go up (holding or buying). Short: betting the price will go down (selling or borrowing-to-sell). A long/short ratio of 1.3 means there are 1.3 longs for every 1 short.
mark price — The reference price used to calculate funding payments and margin on perpetual futures. Usually close to the spot price but may differ slightly to prevent manipulation.
open interest — The total value of all open positions (longs and shorts) in a perpetual contract. Rising open interest signals increasing leverage and conviction; falling open interest signals de-risking.
perpetual futures (perps) — Leveraged contracts that track a cryptocurrency or stock price but never expire (unlike traditional futures). Traders can hold positions indefinitely, paying periodic funding rates.
spot market — The cash market where you buy and hold an actual asset at today's price, as opposed to futures where you agree to a price for future delivery.
swap — In finance, a contract exchanging one cash flow for another (e.g., fixed for floating interest). In regulatory terms, 'swaps' have specific oversight requirements; some crypto contracts are debated whether they are 'swaps' or not.
