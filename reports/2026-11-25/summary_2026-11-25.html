The main reason people got worried was that the US government released inflation data showing prices going up faster than people expected. 07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis points — One hundredth of one percent (0.01%); used to describe very small percentage changes, often in interest rates or funding rates. One basis point = 0.01%.
Breadth — The percentage of coins or tokens on an exchange that traded higher on the day; breadth above 60% is considered bullish (most coins moving up) and below 40% is bearish (most coins moving down).
Core PCE — The Federal Reserve's preferred measure of inflation; it tracks the month-to-month and year-to-year change in prices paid by consumers, excluding the more volatile food and energy sectors.
Fear & Greed Index — A sentiment measure ranging from 0 (Extreme Fear) to 100 (Extreme Greed) that attempts to capture market emotion using data like Bitcoin price momentum, volatility, trading volume, and social media sentiment; 13 is considered extremely fearful.
Funding rate — A periodic payment between traders holding long and short positions on perpetual futures contracts; positive funding means long traders pay short traders (bullish signal), negative means shorts pay longs (bearish signal). Rates are typically expressed as a decimal (e.g., 0.0776%) and charged every 8 hours on most exchanges.
Long position / Long trader — A trader who has bought a contract betting the price will rise; they profit if price goes up and lose if it goes down.
Long/short ratio — The ratio of traders holding long positions (betting on price increases) to traders holding short positions (betting on price decreases); a ratio above 1.0 means more traders are bullish (more longs than shorts).
Mark price — The price used by perpetual futures exchanges to calculate profits, losses, and liquidations; it is typically a weighted average of trades across multiple exchanges to prevent manipulation.
Open interest (OI) — The total number of open futures contracts (measured in coins or USD equivalent) for a given trading pair; it reflects the total money locked in that derivatives market and can signal whether traders are increasing or decreasing leverage.
Perpetual futures / Perpetuals / Perps — Futures contracts with no expiration date that allow traders to bet on crypto prices using leverage (borrowed money); they differ from regular futures which expire on set dates.
Short position / Short trader — A trader who has sold a contract (often by borrowing the asset) betting the price will fall; they profit if price goes down and lose if it goes up.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of market buy orders filled (aggressive buying) to market sell orders filled (aggressive selling); a ratio above 1.0 means more traders are aggressively buying than selling.
Top trader long/short ratio — The long-to-short positioning ratio specifically among the largest traders on the exchange (usually the top 20 accounts by notional size); it can signal where big money is positioning.
Trading volume — The total value of assets traded on an exchange over a time period (measured in USD or the base currency); higher volume typically indicates more conviction and liquidity.
