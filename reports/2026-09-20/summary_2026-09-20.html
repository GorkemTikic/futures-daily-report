07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Annualized percentage rate (APR) — The yearly cost or return expressed as a percentage. Funding rates reported here as APR show what it would cost to hold a leveraged position for a full year.
Arbitrage (arb) — Exploiting price differences between venues (e.g., buying Bitcoin at one exchange and selling at another) or products (spot vs. futures) for near-risk-free profit.
Automated Market Maker (AMM) — A smart contract that lets traders swap cryptocurrencies against a liquidity pool rather than an order book. Used heavily in decentralized finance.
Basis — The difference between a futures contract's price and the spot price of the underlying asset. Positive basis (futures higher) encourages arbitrage selling.
Funding rate — A periodic fee exchanged between long and short traders on leveraged contracts to keep futures prices aligned with spot. Positive rates mean longs pay shorts (bullish sentiment); negative means shorts pay longs (bearish).
Layer 1 (L1) — A foundational blockchain network (e.g., Bitcoin, Ethereum). Distinguished from Layer 2 (L2) solutions that batch transactions to reduce costs.
Leverage / Leveraged position — Borrowing capital to control a larger position than your own cash allows. A 2x position means $1 of capital controls $2 worth of an asset; losses and gains are doubled.
Liquidation — Automatic forced closure of a leveraged position when losses exceed collateral, triggered at mark price. Liquidation cascades occur when one forced sale triggers others.
Long/short ratio — The proportion of traders betting price will rise (long) versus those betting it will fall (short). >1 means more longs; <1 means more shorts.
Mark price — The exchange's calculated fair price for a futures contract, used to trigger liquidations. Differs slightly from the last traded price to prevent manipulation.
Net flow — Daily inflows to an ETF minus outflows. Positive net flow (more buying) signals institutional confidence; negative (selling) suggests caution.
Open interest (OI) — The total notional value of all unfilled orders across all traders on a venue. Growth shows traders adding positions; shrinkage shows them closing.
Open-interest change — Growth or shrinkage of OI during a time period. Indicates whether traders are building (adding) or reducing (closing) positions.
Perpetual futures (perpetuals) — A never-expiring leveraged contract that tracks spot price closely via funding rates. Common on crypto exchanges; no settlement date.
Spot ETF — An exchange-traded fund holding actual Bitcoin or Ethereum (not futures). U.S.-regulated products with daily transparency and institutional custody.
Spot price — The current market price for immediate purchase. The 'real' price in the cash market (as opposed to derivatives prices).
Staking — Locking cryptocurrency in a Proof-of-Stake network to validate transactions and earn yield. Stakers replace miners in securing the blockchain.
Tokenized stocks — Traditional company shares issued as blockchain tokens, preserving dividend and voting rights. Can trade 24/7 via smart contracts on public blockchains.
Volume — The total notional value of trades executed in a time period, expressed in USD millions. Higher volume usually means tighter bid-ask spreads and deeper liquidity.
Whipsaw — A sudden sharp price move in one direction immediately followed by a reversal, catching traders off-guard and triggering stop-losses.
