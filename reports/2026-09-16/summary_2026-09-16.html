07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis point — One-hundredth of a percent (0.01%). Used to describe small changes in interest rates or percentages. A 0.25% rate increase equals 25 basis points.
Bitcoin — The first and largest cryptocurrency by market value. It is a digital currency that runs on a network of computers and records transactions on a public ledger called blockchain.
ETF (exchange-traded fund) — A fund that tracks the price of an asset (like Bitcoin or gold) and trades on regular stock exchanges. Investors can buy and sell ETF shares like stocks.
Ethereum — A blockchain network and its associated cryptocurrency. It enables programmable applications and smart contracts on top of a public ledger, similar to Bitcoin but more flexible.
Funding rate — A fee that traders with leveraged positions pay to each other every few hours (often 8 hours on crypto exchanges). When funding rates are positive, traders betting on price increases pay those betting on decreases. High positive rates signal bullish sentiment; high negative rates signal bearish sentiment.
Leveraged position — A bet placed with borrowed money, allowing a trader to control a larger amount than they deposited. For example, 10x leverage means controlling $10,000 worth of Bitcoin while depositing only $1,000. Leverage amplifies both gains and losses.
Long/short ratio — A comparison of how many traders are betting on price increases (long) versus price decreases (short). A ratio of 1.53 means 1.53 traders are long for every 1 trader short. Higher ratios indicate bullish sentiment.
Mark price — The current fair-value price of a futures contract, calculated by exchanges using the spot price plus a funding adjustment. It is used to decide if a leveraged position should be forcibly closed (liquidated).
Open interest — The total number of leveraged positions (long and short bets combined) currently open on an exchange. Rising open interest suggests new money entering; falling open interest suggests traders closing positions.
Venue — A crypto exchange or trading platform where futures contracts are bought and sold. In this report, venues include Binance, Bybit, OKX, Bitget, and Gate.
