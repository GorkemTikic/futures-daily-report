07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis point — One hundredth of a percent (0.01%). When interest rates rise by 25 basis points, that is a 0.25% increase.
FOMC — The Federal Open Market Committee—the group inside the U.S. Federal Reserve that decides interest rates and monetary policy.
Funding rate — A periodic payment that long traders pay to short traders (or vice versa) in crypto futures contracts. Positive rates mean longs are paying shorts. High rates signal excessive bullishness and warn of possible liquidations.
Long / short — Long means betting a price will go up; short means betting it will go down. A long/short ratio compares how many traders are long versus short. Ratios above 1.0 mean more bulls than bears.
Mark price — The reference price an exchange uses to calculate profits, losses, and liquidations for futures contracts. It often differs slightly from the last traded price to prevent price manipulation.
Open interest — The total number of open futures or options contracts at an exchange. Rising open interest means new money is entering; falling open interest means positions are closing out.
Open-interest change — The percentage increase or decrease in total open contracts over a time period. A rise suggests fresh capital betting, often accompanied by larger price moves.
Perpetual contract — A crypto futures contract with no expiration date, allowing traders to hold long or short positions indefinitely. Traders pay funding rates to maintain positions instead of paying an upfront premium.
Rate hike — When a central bank (like the Federal Reserve) raises its benchmark interest rate, making borrowing more expensive and savings more rewarding.
