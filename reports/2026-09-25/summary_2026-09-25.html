Most other smaller digital currencies had a pretty good day. The big scary news was that a big exchange called Bitget lost $387 million in a hack. The people running Bitget think North Korea did it. It was a day with good and bad news at the same time — some coins did really well, but serious problems with safety and government support.

The three that mattered most

Bitget suffers $387.5 million security breach; North Korea suspected
Bitget's CEO Gracy Chen says North Korea is very likely behind the theft of $387.5 million in cryptocurrency from the exchange's hot and warm wallets. An attacker faked internal transfer requests to trigger the exchange's authorization process without stealing private keys. Circle and Tether froze approximately $318,000 in stablecoins (USDC and USDT) tied to the hacker's wallet, but the vast majority of stolen funds were swapped into Ethereum—which cannot be frozen. The attack is the largest cryptocurrency theft of 2026 to date.

SEC's Hester Peirce, 'Crypto Mom,' to depart October 2
Hester Peirce, the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission's most vocal crypto advocate, announced she will resign on October 2, 2026, ending her nine-year term and joining Regent University School of Law as an associate professor in November. Her departure will leave the SEC with only two commissioners—a historically shorthanded agency that can still operate with a quorum of two. The timing comes as the agency continues releasing fresh crypto guidance.

U.S. authorities seize $89 million from Tether's banking partner EQIBank
Federal prosecutors seized approximately $89 million from EQIBank, a Dominica-licensed offshore bank that processes funds for Tether and other crypto firms. The seizure targeted accounts at the bank's U.S. payment processor, Capstone, which moved money through Wells Fargo and JPMorgan Chase. Tether confirmed it holds assets at EQIBank but said its exposure amounts to less than 0.034% of its total $187.75 billion in backing—capping potential loss around $64 million. The company said it had no knowledge of Capstone's alleged violations and that its funds remain accessible, posing no immediate threat to USDT's dollar peg.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis trade — Strategy of borrowing crypto at one rate, selling it via futures at a higher price, and pocketing the difference when contracts expire—used by platforms like Ethena to back stablecoin yield
Blacklist / Blacklisting — Stablecoin issuers' ability to freeze or block specific cryptocurrency wallet addresses from holding their coins—used by Circle (USDC) and Tether (USDT) to comply with sanctions and law enforcement
Cold wallet — Offline cryptocurrency storage; most secure but slowest to access funds—typically held by exchanges in vaults or hardware devices not connected to the internet
Fear & Greed Index — A sentiment gauge measuring market emotion from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed); helps traders identify potential reversals when extremes are reached
Funding rate — Interest paid between traders holding opposite sides of perpetual futures contracts—positive rates mean longs pay shorts; negative rates mean shorts pay longs; used to keep perpetual contract prices aligned with spot
Hot wallet — Exchange cryptocurrency storage kept online for quick customer withdrawals; easier to hack than cold storage but necessary for operational liquidity
Leverage — Borrowing money to increase the size of a trading position beyond what a trader's own capital could buy; amplifies both gains and losses
Long / Short — Long = bet that price will go up; short = bet that price will go down; the long/short ratio shows how many traders are bullish vs. bearish on an asset
Mark price — The reference price used to settle perpetual futures contracts and calculate trader profit/loss; designed to track the spot market price but may diverge temporarily
Open interest — The total dollar value of all open (unsettled) futures contracts on an exchange; rising open interest suggests fresh money entering the market, while falling open interest suggests traders closing positions
Open-interest change — The net increase or decrease in open interest over a period; positive change = new leverage being added to the market; negative change = leverage unwinding
Perpetual futures / Perps — Cryptocurrency derivatives contracts that allow traders to bet on price movements with leverage; unlike standard futures, they have no expiration date and use funding rates to stay tethered to spot price
Spot ETF — Exchange-traded fund that holds actual cryptocurrency (Bitcoin or Ethereum) and trades on U.S. stock exchanges; differs from futures-based ETFs because it holds real coins
Stablecoin — Cryptocurrency pegged to hold a fixed value, typically $1 USD; the main ones are USDT (Tether) and USDC (Circle); used for buying/selling crypto and holding value without price risk
Warm wallet — Intermediate cryptocurrency storage between hot wallets (fast, less secure) and cold wallets (slow, most secure); offers a balance of access speed and security
