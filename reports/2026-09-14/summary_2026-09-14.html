🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin and Ethereum each went up about 1.7-1.8% on September 14. The main story was that President Trump said the US-Iran war might end soon, which made people more willing to take risks with crypto — and crypto prices went up. At the same time, big AI company leaders like Sam Altman and Mustafa Suleyman said the AI industry should slow down and be more careful, and this scared people. Computer chip companies in Asia — like Samsung and SK Hynix in Korea, and Zhongji Innolight in Hong Kong — all lost money because people worry about chip demand if AI slows. Finally, Congress was getting ready for a big vote about new crypto rules called the Clarity Act. Some people in the White House like it, but state officials said no. It was a normal day with nothing scary, but there's a lot of big decisions coming.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis points — One hundredth of one percent. If funding rate is 0.01% (0.0001 raw), that is 1 basis point per day.
Caveman summary — A simple, jargon-free explanation of the day's events using everyday words, written for someone with no finance background.
Funding rate — A periodic payment between long and short traders on a futures exchange. Positive funding means longs pay shorts (bullish signal of overheating); negative funding means shorts pay longs (bearish signal). Annualized funding shows what an annual rate would be if daily funding stayed constant.
Long-to-short ratio (long/short ratio) — The proportion of traders betting price will go UP (long) versus DOWN (short). A ratio above 1 means more longs than shorts. Very high ratios (like 2.98 for ETH) signal extreme long leverage and risk of sudden liquidation if price reverses.
Mark price — The 'true' price of a futures contract, used to calculate funding payments and prevent manipulation. It usually tracks spot price but may diverge slightly.
Open interest (OI) — The total amount of unsettled contracts (in coins or dollars) on an exchange. Rising OI = traders adding leverage and conviction; falling OI = positions closing. A stable or rising OI at higher prices signals strong bullish positioning.
Spot market — The market where you buy or sell crypto for immediate delivery (you own it outright). Differs from futures, where you bet on price without owning the asset.
Treasury company (Bitcoin treasury, corporate Bitcoin holder) — A publicly traded company (e.g., Strive, Strategy, Bitmine) that holds significant Bitcoin or Ethereum as a long-term investment, similar to how companies hold cash reserves. Announcements of treasury purchases or sales move markets.
Venue (exchange) — A cryptocurrency trading platform where futures are bought and sold (e.g., Binance, Bybit, OKX, Bitget, Gate.io).
Vesting — A lock-up period on crypto tokens or shares. A vested amount cannot be sold until the lock-up ends. Used to align incentives and prevent sudden dumps.
