🗿 The day, explained simply

On October 12, Bitcoin and Ethereum both dropped about 2–3% because tensions between countries in the Middle East and Ukraine made people nervous about oil prices going up. When oil prices rise, people worry about inflation and tend to sell risky stuff like crypto, so both coins fell. At the same time, US investment funds that hold Bitcoin and Ethereum were pulling their money out (about $1.2 billion left in just one week), adding more selling pressure. On the bright side, two surprising things happened: Blur, a marketplace where people trade NFTs (special digital pictures), announced it's closing down, which made its token BLUR crash, and Gitcoin (GTC) jumped way up when a Korean exchange announced it would let people trade it there. Overall it was a worrying day—the market was broadly down, most coins were losing money, and the big traders were getting less confident.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The difference between the spot price (cash market) and the futures price. A positive basis means futures trade above spot (bullish); a negative basis means futures trade below spot (bearish).
Breadth — The percentage of all assets that moved up (or down) on a given day. Breadth of 10% up means only 1 in 10 tokens gained value—a sign of concentrated moves and weak overall participation.
Fear and Greed Index — A daily sentiment gauge (0–100 scale) that measures whether crypto markets are dominated by fear (0–30), neutral (31–70), or greed (71–100). Used to spot potential reversals when sentiment reaches extremes.
Funding rate — The interest rate paid by traders holding leveraged positions (long or short) to the opposite side. Positive funding means long traders pay shorts (bullish signal), negative means shorts pay longs (bearish). Rates are paid every 8 hours on major exchanges.
Long/short ratio — The ratio of traders betting on price rises (longs) versus those betting on declines (shorts). A ratio above 1.0 means more longs exist; below 1.0 means more shorts. Extreme ratios can signal exhaustion (too many on one side).
Mark price — The reference price used to calculate trading profits/losses and trigger liquidations on perpetual (leveraged) contracts. It typically averages the spot price and futures price to prevent manipulation.
Open interest — The total dollar value of all open leveraged positions (long and short combined) on an exchange at a given time. Rising open interest signals more leverage entering the market; falling open interest means positions are being closed or liquidated.
Open interest change — The percentage increase or decrease in total open interest over a day. A decline often signals deleveraging (forced or voluntary position closures) and can precede sharp price moves.
Stablecoin — A cryptocurrency designed to hold a fixed value (usually $1 USD) by maintaining reserves or using algorithms. Common examples are USDC (dollar-backed) and USDT (dollar-backed Tether). Used as a safe harbor in volatile crypto markets.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of market-buy orders (takers) versus market-sell orders. A ratio above 1.0 means more aggressive buying; below 1.0 means more aggressive selling. Reflects immediate momentum and sentiment.
Top position ratio (top trader long/short) — The long-to-short ratio among the largest traders on an exchange. Often more reliable than overall account ratios because it shows whether institutions are bullish or bearish.
