🗿 The day, explained simply

On Wednesday, the US government announced that prices went up faster than expected—the inflation number came in at 3.7%, which was higher than forecasters predicted. This scared people because it might force the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates again. When interest rates go up, people move money away from risky things like Bitcoin and Ethereum to safer places. Bitcoin fell to about $81,641, losing almost 2% of its value. Ethereum fell more—about 3%. The stock market fell too (the big US stock indexes dropped 1–1.8%). In Asia, computer chip companies crashed even harder because of the interest rate worry and because China is making their own memory chips now. Most coins went down: only about 13 out of every 100 tokens traded up on Binance. It was a worrying day for crypto traders.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — In this report, 'utc-day basis' means the price movement is measured over a full UTC calendar day (00:00–23:59 UTC).
Breadth (market breadth) — The percentage of all traded perpetual contracts that went up on the day. Low breadth (like 12.9%) means most coins fell; high breadth means a broad rally across the market.
Core CPI — Core inflation is the headline consumer price index minus energy and food prices, which tend to be volatile. Core inflation shows the underlying price trend.
Funding rate — A small payment that long traders (those betting price will rise) pay to short traders (those betting price will fall) every 8 hours on futures exchanges. Positive rates favor shorts; negative rates favor longs. Annualized rates show what the rate would be if kept constant for a year.
Headline CPI — The total change in consumer prices, including food and energy. It's often more volatile than core inflation but represents the full cost-of-living picture for ordinary people.
Long position / long — A bet that the price will rise. Long traders profit if the asset goes up; they lose if it goes down.
Long/short ratio — The number of traders betting the price will rise (longs) divided by the number betting it will fall (shorts). A ratio above 1.0 means more longs than shorts (bullish); below 1.0 means more shorts (bearish).
Mark price — The reference price used to calculate profit and loss on futures contracts, typically a blend of the spot price and funding-weighted futures prices. It prevents flash crashes from liquidating traders unfairly.
Open interest — The total amount of money (in USD) or coins tied up in open futures contracts on an exchange. Rising open interest with falling price can mean shorts are piling in; rising with rising price means long conviction.
Short position / short — A bet that the price will fall. Short traders profit if the asset goes down; they lose if it goes up.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of buying volume to selling volume among traders who take existing orders (takers). A ratio above 1.0 means more aggressive buying; below 1.0 means more aggressive selling.
Top-tier trader ratio — The long/short ratio specifically among the largest traders and whales on an exchange, not all accounts. It shows what the biggest players believe.
