🦴 Caveman mode
🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin and Ethereum both went up a little less than 1% on Saturday during normal trading. The total amount of money tied up in Bitcoin trades actually shrank, even though the cost of borrowing money to trade Bitcoin stayed very expensive (people trading with borrowed money are paying 11% per year). Institutional money (big funds and investment companies) bought more Bitcoin on Friday, which shows they still want it. Three smaller, riskier coins shot up a lot (one went up 96%) but nobody knows exactly why. Brazil announced it's blocking stablecoins from certain ways of moving money between countries starting in October. Overall, it was a calm Saturday with no major shocks or panics.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis (UTC-day or rolling-24h) — The time window over which a price or volume move is measured. UTC-day means one calendar day (00:00–23:59 UTC). Rolling-24h means the most recent 24-hour period, regardless of date boundaries.
Funding rate — The interest rate traders using leverage pay each other to hold positions on a futures exchange. When funding is positive and high, longs are paying shorts—a sign leverage is expensive and trader sentiment is bullish. Typically charged every 8 hours.
Leverage — Borrowing money to amplify the size of a trade. For example, 10x leverage means you control $10 of assets with $1 of your own capital. Higher leverage increases both gains and losses.
Liquidation — Forced closure of a leveraged position when the trader's margin (collateral) is exhausted due to losses. Liquidations can trigger sudden price moves as stop-loss orders cascade.
Long-to-short ratio (or long/short ratio) — The ratio of bullish (long) to bearish (short) accounts or positions on an exchange. A ratio above 1 means more traders are betting on price increases; below 1 means more are betting on decreases. Balanced ratios near 1 indicate mixed sentiment.
Mark price — The official settlement price used by an exchange to calculate profit/loss on futures contracts, typically the index price of the underlying asset (e.g., Bitcoin or Ethereum). Different from the last trade price.
Open interest — The total value of all active futures contracts on an exchange at a single moment. A rising open interest means more money is being deployed; falling open interest means traders are closing positions and reducing leverage.
Open-interest change — The percentage move in total open interest over a time period (e.g., one day). Falling OI with prices rising suggests a slow-down in leverage despite price momentum.
Stablecoin — A cryptocurrency designed to hold a fixed value (usually $1 USD per coin) through reserves or algorithmic means. Examples: USDC, USDT. Used for settlement and to avoid price volatility.
Venue — A cryptocurrency exchange or trading platform where futures contracts are offered (e.g., Binance, Bybit, OKX).
Volume (quote or base) — The total value or quantity of assets traded over a period. Quote volume is in USD; base volume is in the asset itself (e.g., BTC).
