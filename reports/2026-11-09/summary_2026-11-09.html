🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin went up a little bit on November 9—less than 1%. Ethereum went up just a tiny bit too (about 0.3%). Most other cryptocurrencies did okay, with about 83 out of 100 going up. But here's the worry: lots of people who own Bitcoin through investments—like ETFs that regular people buy through their banks—were selling and pulling out billions of dollars. Wall Street in New York announced they would build a new place to trade company stocks using blockchain, and they picked Avalanche to run the computers. Avalanche's price jumped 10%. The hackers who stole billions from Binance earlier in the year are still hiding the money they took by using fake coins. Chip-making companies in South Korea bounced back a little bit after losing a lot the week before. Overall it was a day of good news for the future but sad money flowing out.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Breadth — The percentage of cryptocurrencies in the market that moved up in price on a given day. High breadth (above 80%) signals broad strength across coins; low breadth signals most coins are falling.
Funding rate — The periodic payment between long and short traders on futures exchanges. A positive rate means longs pay shorts (signaling belief prices will rise). An annualized rate shows what that rate equals over a full year.
Long/short ratio — The proportion of traders holding bullish (long) positions versus bearish (short) positions. A ratio above 1 means more traders are betting prices will rise; below 1 means more are betting on a fall.
Mark price — The price used for calculating profits and losses on futures contracts, often a weighted average of recent spot and perpetual futures prices designed to prevent manipulation.
Open interest — The total number of active futures contracts in the market at a given time. Rising open interest means traders are opening new positions; falling open interest means traders are closing positions.
Open-interest change — The percentage increase or decrease in total open contracts over a time period. Falling OI suggests traders are exiting positions and reducing leverage.
Spot ETF inflow/outflow — Movement of investor money into or out of regulated Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs. Inflows signal institutional demand; outflows signal selling pressure or portfolio rebalancing.
Taker buy/sell ratio — A measure of aggressive buying versus selling. Above 1.0 means more buyers are taking (buying) existing sell orders; below 1.0 means more sellers are taking (selling) existing buy orders.
Top-position ratio — The long/short ratio among the largest-account traders only, not all traders. Signals conviction among institutions and whales.
