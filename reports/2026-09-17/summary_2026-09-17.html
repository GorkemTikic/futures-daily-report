07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Annualized percentage (for funding rates) — A funding rate that compounds eight times per year is multiplied by 8 and shown as an annual percentage. For example, a 0.00006 rate every 8 hours becomes 6.9 percent annualized, meaning if you held the same position all year, you would pay that much.
Basis — The time period over which a price or change is measured. 'UTC-day' basis means the price changed from the start of the UTC calendar day (00:00 UTC) to the snapshot time. 'Rolling-24h' means the most recent 24 hours of trading, which may not align with calendar days.
Funding rate — A fee that traders holding leveraged positions pay or receive every 8 hours. A positive funding rate means traders betting on price increases pay traders betting on decreases—a signal that more traders are bullish. Higher rates signal stronger bullish positioning and increased leverage.
Long/short ratio — The number of traders betting prices will go UP (long) divided by the number betting prices will go DOWN (short). A ratio above 1.0 means more bullish traders. A 2.0 ratio means twice as many traders are betting upward.
Mark price — The reference price used by a derivatives exchange to measure profits and losses for leveraged trades. It's calculated from spot markets and futures prices to prevent traders from pushing the price too far away from real-world value.
Open interest — The total amount of money locked in leveraged positions (futures and perpetuals) across all traders on an exchange. Rising open interest means traders are adding leverage; falling open interest means they are closing positions or reducing bets.
Perpetual contract / perpetual futures — A leveraged derivative that lets you bet on price movement without an expiration date, unlike traditional futures. You can hold the position indefinitely as long as you have collateral and pay funding fees.
Spot ETF — An exchange-traded fund that physically holds the asset (e.g., Bitcoin or Ethereum) and trades on stock exchanges. When you buy a spot ETF, you own a claim on the actual underlying asset.
Tokenized securities / tokenized stocks — Digital versions of real company stock that trade on blockchain venues instead of traditional stock exchanges. A tokenized Apple share represents the same ownership rights and dividends as a traditional Apple share but can trade 24/7 on blockchain networks.
Venue — A cryptocurrency exchange or trading platform where you can buy and sell crypto assets.
