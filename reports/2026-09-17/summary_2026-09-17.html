07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Funding rate — A periodic fee (every 8 hours) paid between traders on futures exchanges. When funding is positive, long (buy) traders pay short (sell) traders, indicating bullish sentiment. A high funding rate suggests traders are aggressively betting on price increases.
Long/Short ratio — The number of traders holding buy positions (longs) divided by traders holding sell positions (shorts). A ratio above 1.0 means more traders are betting on prices rising; below 1.0 means more are betting on prices falling.
Mark price — A futures exchange's reference price for a contract, calculated from spot price and funding to prevent flash crashes. It is the price used to calculate if your position should be closed due to losses (liquidation).
Open interest — The total number of open futures contracts (buy orders matched with sell orders) at an exchange. A rising open interest means new traders are entering; falling means traders are exiting.
Open-interest change — The percentage change in total open contracts over a day, showing whether traders are opening or closing positions. Positive means new shorts and longs are entering; negative means they are exiting.
Perpetual futures — A cryptocurrency futures contract with no expiry date that trades spot-like but with leverage (borrowed money). Traders pay or receive funding fees based on sentiment; prices are tethered to the real spot price by these fees.
Real-world assets (RWAs) — Physical or traditional financial assets (stocks, bonds, commodities, real estate) that are represented as digital tokens on a blockchain, enabling them to trade 24/7 on blockchain venues.
Stablecoin — A cryptocurrency designed to hold a steady price (usually $1 USD) by being backed by reserves (cash, short-term debt, or an algorithmic mechanism) so it can be used for everyday transactions and savings.
Tokenized securities — Real company stocks or bonds represented as digital tokens on a blockchain, allowing them to trade on blockchain-native venues without traditional stock exchange infrastructure.
