The biggest thing that happened today was that Binance (the world's largest place to trade Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies) opened back up for people to take their money out after hackers stole $6.3 billion from it on October 29. 07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Breadth (market breadth) — The percentage of cryptocurrencies that rose in price on a given day. A reading above 50% suggests overall strength; below 50% suggests weakness. On November 1, 52.6% of Binance perpetuals were up.
Fear and Greed Index — A sentiment gauge measuring how fearful or greedy cryptocurrency market participants are feeling, on a scale from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed). November 1 closed at 34 (Fear), up from 30 the day before.
Funding rate — The fee leveraged traders pay to maintain long or short bets on perpetual contracts. Positive funding means traders betting up are paying; negative means traders betting down are paying. Higher rates encourage position-taking in that direction.
Long position — A bet that a cryptocurrency's price will go up. A trader 'goes long' by borrowing money to buy more than they can afford, betting on price increases.
Long/short ratio — The number of traders betting on price increases (long) divided by those betting on price decreases (short). A ratio of 2.5 means 2.5 long traders for every 1 short trader, indicating strong bullish positioning.
Mark price — The reference price used for perpetual contracts, calculated from the spot price and funding rates. Used to determine gains/losses on leveraged bets. Different from last-traded price.
MEV (Maximal Extractable Value) — The profit that validators or miners can make by choosing the order and inclusion of transactions in a block. On November 1, Ethereum validators captured about $30 million in MEV profits from the hackers' rushed trades.
Open interest — The total number of cryptocurrency contracts (or coins) that are currently open in perpetual futures markets. Increasing open interest suggests growing conviction; decreasing suggests traders are closing positions.
Open-interest change — The percentage rise or fall in total open contracts on a given day, indicating whether traders are entering new leveraged positions (positive change) or exiting (negative change).
Perpetual contract — A leveraged futures contract with no expiration date, allowing traders to bet on cryptocurrency price moves using borrowed money. Perpetuals pay funding rates between longs and shorts to keep the contract price anchored to the spot price.
Short position — A bet that a cryptocurrency's price will go down. A trader 'goes short' by borrowing cryptocurrency to sell it, then hoping to buy it back cheaper.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of aggressive buyer trades to aggressive seller trades that 'take' liquidity on an order book. A ratio below 1.0 means more aggressive selling pressure than buying; above 1.0 means more buying pressure.
Top-trader position ratio (top position ratio) — The long-to-short ratio among the largest/most influential traders on an exchange, not all traders. Divergence from the overall long-short ratio can signal whether whales are betting differently than the broader market.
