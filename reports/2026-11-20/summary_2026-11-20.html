🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin and Ethereum both fell sharply on Friday—Bitcoin dropped about 5% and Ethereum dropped about 8%—because traders worldwide got scared and started selling. The main reason was that the U.S. stock market fell hard (more than 3% for the big companies and over 5% for technology stocks), which made people nervous about all kinds of investments. Anthropic, a company that makes AI technology, had just started trading its stock on Thursday and its share price kept falling Friday, adding to the panic. At the same time, almost all other cryptocurrencies also fell (only about 1 out of 10 coins went up), showing how scared everyone was. It was a very worrying day.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — In futures trading, the difference between the spot price (cash price) and the futures price.
Breadth — The percentage of coins or tokens that ended the trading day higher (up), lower (down), or unchanged, showing how broad the market move was. High upPct means most coins rose; low upPct means most coins fell.
Fear & Greed Index — A sentiment measure ranging from 0 (Extreme Fear) to 100 (Extreme Greed) that reflects how scared or confident crypto traders are. Extreme Fear is when most traders are very worried.
Funding rate — The interest rate that long (bullish) traders pay to short (bearish) traders to hold positions. Negative rates mean shorts are paying longs—a signal that bearish traders are in control. Positive rates mean longs are paying shorts—a signal that bullish traders are dominant.
Long-to-short ratio — The ratio of bullish (long) traders to bearish (short) traders in an account. A ratio of 3:1 means there are three times as many longs as shorts.
Mark price — The reference price used to calculate profits and losses on a futures contract, often a weighted average of recent trades and spot price designed to prevent manipulation.
Open interest — The total number of open (unfilled) futures contracts or derivatives positions at an exchange. Rising open interest means traders are adding new positions; falling open interest means positions are being closed.
Open-interest change — The increase or decrease in the total number of open contracts during the trading day, showing whether traders are adding or removing risk from the market.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of market buy orders (takers buying at the ask) to market sell orders (takers selling at the bid). Above 1.0 means more buyers are taking the ask (aggressive buying); below 1.0 means more sellers are taking the bid (aggressive selling).
Top-trader position ratio — The long-to-short ratio among only the largest traders (not all accounts), showing whether the biggest money is betting on prices rising or falling.
