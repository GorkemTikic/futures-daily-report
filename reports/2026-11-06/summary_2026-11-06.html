🗿 The day, explained simply

Friday was a mixed but worrying day in crypto. Bitcoin went down less than one percent while Ethereum barely moved. The big news was that the US government released employment numbers—companies hired 165,000 workers, way more than people expected (they thought 65,000). When lots of new jobs appear, the Federal Reserve might raise interest rates, which makes people sell their riskier investments like Bitcoin. At the same time, artificial-intelligence stocks dropped sharply, so investors sold crypto to get cash. Over half a billion dollars came out of Bitcoin and Ethereum funds. It was a worrying day.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The difference between the futures price and the spot price. A positive basis (futures higher) signals long positioning; negative basis signals short positioning.
Breadth — The percentage of assets in a market that are trading up on the day. High breadth (over 70%) indicates broad-based strength.
Fear & Greed Index — A sentiment indicator ranging from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed). Readings below 30 signal fear, above 70 signal greed.
Funding rate — A periodic payment between long and short traders on futures exchanges. Positive rates mean longs pay shorts; negative rates mean shorts pay longs. Higher rates indicate more leverage and risk.
Long/short ratio — The ratio of open long positions to open short positions. A ratio above 1 means more traders are betting on price increases; below 1 means more are betting on decreases.
Mark price — The reference price used to calculate funding payments and mark unrealized gains/losses on futures. It smooths out spot price swings to prevent manipulation.
Open interest (OI) — The total value of all open futures contracts at an exchange. Rising OI often indicates new money entering the market; falling OI suggests positions are closing.
Open interest change — The day-to-day change in total open contracts. Positive change means new long or short contracts are opening; negative change means positions are closing.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of buy-side aggressive orders (takers) to sell-side aggressive orders. Above 1 signals more aggressive buying; below 1 signals more aggressive selling.
Top position ratio — The long-to-short ratio among the largest traders at an exchange. A high ratio (e.g., 2.0) means the biggest accounts are heavily net long.
