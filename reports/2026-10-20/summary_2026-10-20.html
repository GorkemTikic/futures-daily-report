🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin and Ethereum both went up about 1.3–1.7% on Tuesday. The main reason: BlackRock (a really big investment company) bought $665 million of Bitcoin—the most they've bought in almost a year—and JPMorgan (a huge bank) announced that Bitcoin could go up 10% per year for the next 10–15 years, which made people more hopeful about crypto. But there's bad news too: hackers who stole money from a protocol called Drift back in April are still moving the stolen Ethereum through mixer services (like a money-laundering tool). The US stock market went up slightly. Most other cryptocurrencies also went up. Overall, it was a good day for crypto with strong buying from big institutions, but security worries didn't disappear.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The time period or reference used for measuring price changes. 'UTC-day' means a full calendar day in Coordinated Universal Time (UTC), 00:00–23:59.
Breadth — The percentage of assets moving up vs. down on the day. 83.86% up means that on Binance perps, about 84 out of 100 contracts closed higher than they opened, showing market-wide strength.
Fear & Greed Index — A sentiment score from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed) that measures overall crypto market psychology. 55 is 'greed'—investors are optimistic but not panicking.
Funding rate — A periodic payment between traders on perpetual futures. When funding rates are positive, bullish traders (longs) pay bearish traders (shorts) to hold their positions, signaling strong bullish sentiment. Higher rates mean more bullish confidence.
Long/short ratio (account-level and top-position ratio) — Comparison of bullish accounts (betting price goes up) to bearish accounts (betting price goes down). A ratio above 1.0 means more bulls than bears. 1.83 means 1.83 bulls for every 1 bear. 'Top-position ratio' focuses on the largest traders; 'account-level' includes all traders.
Mark price — The reference price used to calculate funding on perpetual futures, derived from the spot price and forward curve. It prevents manipulation and marks unrealized gains/losses on open positions.
Open interest (OI) — The total dollar value of all open positions (longs and shorts combined) on a perpetual futures market. Increasing OI means traders are adding leverage to the market; declining OI means positions are being closed. 'OI change' shows how much this grew or shrank during the day.
Perpetual futures (perps) — A type of cryptocurrency derivatives contract with no expiration date. Traders can hold a bullish or bearish position indefinitely as long as they meet margin requirements. They track the spot price closely via funding rates and mark price.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of aggressive buy orders (market buys that take liquidity) to sell orders. Above 1.0 means more aggressive buying; below 1.0 means more aggressive selling. Indicates short-term momentum direction.
Volume (quote basis) — The total dollar value of all contracts traded in a given period (versus 'base volume,' which counts coins). 'Quote basis' means the dollar amounts are summed; higher volume indicates more trading activity and liquidity.
