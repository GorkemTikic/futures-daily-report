07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Annualized funding rate — The annual percentage cost for traders borrowing money to hold a leveraged position. If the rate is 10% annualized, a trader pays roughly 10% per year to hold a long position, or receives 10% per year if shorting. High rates signal aggressive bullish betting; negative rates signal bearish betting.
Bear — An investor or trader who believes a price will fall.
Bitcoin bridge — A program that lets Bitcoin move to other blockchains (like Ethereum). For example, you can deposit real Bitcoin and get syBTC on Ethereum; later you can swap syBTC back for real Bitcoin. A bridge exploit is when a hacker tricks the bridge into minting fake Bitcoin replacements.
Bull — An investor or trader who believes a price will rise.
Cloture vote — A Senate procedure to end debate and force a vote. Requires 60 votes to pass. If it passes, the Senate can vote on the main bill.
Collateral — Assets you deposit to borrow money or trade with leverage. If your position loses too much, the platform sells your collateral to recover the loan.
Crypto (cryptocurrency) — Digital money that runs on a blockchain (a distributed ledger) rather than being controlled by a central bank. Bitcoin and Ethereum are cryptocurrencies.
ETF flows — The net amount of investor money flowing into or out of an exchange-traded fund (ETF) on a given day. Large inflows can signal institutional buying; large outflows signal selling.
Ethereum — A blockchain and cryptocurrency launched in 2015. Unlike Bitcoin, Ethereum can run programs (smart contracts) on it. Its token is called Ether (ETH).
Funding rate — The interest rate traders pay to hold a leveraged position. Positive rates mean longs pay shorts; negative rates mean shorts pay longs.
Futures (or perpetual contracts) — A standardized bet that lets you wager on whether a crypto price will go up or down, with leverage (borrowed money). Unlike traditional futures that expire on a date, perpetual contracts can stay open indefinitely as long as you maintain collateral.
Hard fork — A major upgrade to a blockchain that changes the rules—old versions are no longer compatible. The term is sometimes used to refer to ending a chain entirely (as mentioned with Lisk).
Leverage (or margin) — Borrowing money to trade a larger position than you can afford outright. For example, 10x leverage means you control 10 times the amount of collateral you put down. It magnifies both gains and losses.
Liquidation — When a leveraged position loses so much value that the exchange automatically sells it to recover the borrowed money. The trader loses their collateral.
Mark price — The fair price of a futures contract calculated by the exchange using spot prices across venues, index prices, and time decay. It's used to calculate whether a leveraged position should be liquidated. It differs from the last trade price to prevent manipulation.
Open interest (OI) — The total number of unfilled futures contracts (longs + shorts) across all traders on an exchange. Rising OI suggests new leverage is entering the market; falling OI suggests positions are being closed.
Perpetual contract (or perpetual swap) — A futures contract with no expiration date that lets you bet on price direction with leverage. You pay or receive funding periodically to stay long or short.
Short (or short position) — A bet that a price will fall. If you short Bitcoin at $76,000 and the price drops to $75,000, you profit. If it rises to $77,000, you lose.
Short squeeze — A sharp price rally caused by traders who shorted a token being forced to buy to close their bets (to avoid liquidation), which pushes the price even higher and forces more shorts to liquidate—a feedback loop.
Stablecoin — A cryptocurrency designed to maintain a stable price—usually pegged 1:1 to the US dollar or another fiat currency. USDC (run by Circle) and USDT (run by Tether) are major examples.
Staking — Locking up crypto tokens to earn rewards. On some blockchains, stakers help secure the network and earn new tokens as payment. On exchanges like Coincheck, staking is a simpler concept: you hold a token and the exchange pays you interest.
Trading volume — The total dollar (or coin) amount traded on an exchange in a given time period. High volume suggests active trading and good liquidity; low volume suggests fewer buyers and sellers.
USDC — A stablecoin issued by Circle that holds a 1:1 value with the US dollar. It's backed by US treasury bills and bank deposits.
Venue — An exchange or marketplace where crypto futures are traded. This report covers Binance, Bybit, OKX, Bitget, and Gate.
