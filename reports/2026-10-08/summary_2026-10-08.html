🗿 The day, explained simply

On October 8, a top Federal Reserve official named Waller said the Fed would probably need to raise interest rates more to get inflation under control. This scared traders because higher interest rates make borrowing expensive and push down prices of risky bets like Bitcoin. So traders started selling—Bitcoin fell 1.9% to $81,711 and Ethereum fell 3.8% to $2,474. A massive pile of positions got forced closed (over $1 billion in liquidations). People pulled money out of Bitcoin and Ethereum investment funds ($484.9 million out of Bitcoin alone, the worst day since June). The US government also moved $1 billion in Bitcoin that it had seized from hackers years ago, just moving it to new storage—not selling. Only 1 in 5 crypto bets were winning that day. It was a worrying day for crypto markets.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Funding rate — A periodic fee (usually paid every 8 hours) between traders betting prices go up (longs) and traders betting prices go down (shorts). When funding is positive and high (e.g., 10.95% annualized), longs pay shorts, signaling bullish sentiment and attracting shorters. When negative (e.g., −5.5%), shorts pay longs, signaling bearish sentiment.
Liquidation — When a trader's leveraged position is automatically closed by the exchange because losses exceed their collateral. Liquidations are forced sales that occur rapidly and can push prices down further.
Long-short ratio — The ratio of traders betting prices will go up (longs) versus traders betting prices will go down (shorts). A ratio of 1.69:1 means there are 1.69 long positions for every short position—a bullish lean. A ratio below 1.0 indicates more shorts than longs (bearish lean).
Mark price — The official price on a futures exchange used to calculate profit/loss and determine liquidations. It is typically derived from spot price and adjusted by funding to prevent manipulation of the closing price.
Market breadth — The percentage of cryptocurrencies in a market (by count) that rose in value during the day. When only 20.7% of perpetuals were up, it means 79.3% fell or stayed flat—a broad, risk-off signal.
Open interest — The total amount of capital currently locked in leveraged bets (longs and shorts combined) in a market. Rising open interest means more money is entering bets; falling open interest means traders are closing positions and removing money.
Open-interest change — The percentage increase or decrease in total open interest from the start of a day to the end. A −6.4% change means 6.4% of the capital in leveraged bets was withdrawn, signaling deleveraging and reduced conviction.
Strategic Bitcoin Reserve — A U.S. government program (established by executive order) that holds seized and forfeited Bitcoin indefinitely as a strategic asset rather than selling it. The reserve acts as a long-term store of value similar to gold reserves.
Taker buy-sell ratio — A measure of buying versus selling pressure among traders who take immediate deals at current prices. A ratio of 0.93 means slightly more aggressive selling; a ratio above 1.0 means more aggressive buying.
Top-trader long-short ratio — The ratio of long versus short positions among the largest account holders only. This is often used as a proxy for 'smart money' positioning, as the biggest players typically have better information.
Uptober — Informal nickname for October, reflecting Bitcoin's and crypto's historically strong seasonal performance in that month. The term gained popularity as traders watched for expected October gains after weak Septembers.
