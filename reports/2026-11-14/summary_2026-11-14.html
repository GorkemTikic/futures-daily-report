07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Capitulation — Widespread panic selling by the last remaining holders of losing positions, often marked by extreme volume and emotion-driven decisions. Capitulation is often a sign of a local market bottom.
Fear and Greed Index — A daily market sentiment gauge from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed). Values below 25 indicate extreme fear; above 75 indicate extreme greed. Nov 14's reading of 13 reflected extreme panic.
Funding rate — The periodic cost paid by traders holding leveraged positions in perpetual futures contracts. Positive funding means long traders pay short traders (bullish overhang); negative funding means shorts pay longs (bearish overhang). Annualized percentage = funding rate × 365.
Liquidation — Forced automatic closure of a leveraged position when losses exceed the trader's collateral (margin). Large liquidations at key price levels often trigger cascading sell pressure.
Long-to-short ratio — The ratio of open long contracts to open short contracts on a trading symbol. A ratio of 3.2:1 means 3.2 times more longs than shorts, indicating bullish positioning. A falling ratio signals traders unwinding long bets.
Market breadth — The percentage of all trading pairs that moved up, down, or stayed flat in a given period. Market breadth of 6% up means only 6 out of every 100 coins gained value; breadth under 20% signals weakness or capitulation.
Open interest (OI) — The total value or count of active leveraged contracts (longs + shorts) on an exchange or symbol. Falling open interest indicates deleveraging (traders closing positions); rising OI indicates new leverage entering.
Perpetual futures / Perps — Leveraged derivative contracts that trade 24/7 and never expire. No settlement date; traders pay or receive periodic funding rates to keep positions open. Allow betting on any asset without owning it.
Taker buy-to-sell ratio — The ratio of aggressive buy orders to aggressive sell orders on an exchange. A ratio above 1 indicates more aggressive buying; below 1 indicates more aggressive selling. 0.90 on Bitcoin signals more buyers are capitulating than new buyers entering.
Top-trader long-to-short ratio (top-position ratio) — The long-to-short positioning ratio among the largest traders (top tier of account sizes). Often diverges from the retail ratio; a lower top-trader ratio than retail average signals smart money is less bullish than the crowd.
