🗿 The day, explained simply

On Wednesday, October 7th, Bitcoin and Ethereum both dropped: Bitcoin fell from about $85,500 to $83,300 (down 2.6%), and Ethereum fell from about $2,697 to $2,573 (down 4.6%). This happened because the Federal Reserve — the big government bank that controls how expensive it is to borrow money — released minutes from a meeting at 6 PM, and traders thought that meant interest rates were staying high for longer. A famous researcher also warned that artificial intelligence might be able to crack the secret codes that protect people's crypto wallets faster than quantum computers would. That scared people more. Out of all the cryptocurrencies trading today, only 15% of them went up; the rest went down. This was a worrying day for people who own crypto.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The time period used for daily performance and funding calculations—either calendar day (utc-day) or rolling 24 hours (rolling-24h). Different bases show slightly different results.
ETF inflows and outflows — The net amount of money flowing into (inflows) or out of (outflows) exchange-traded funds. Large inflows to Bitcoin ETFs signal institutional demand; outflows suggest profit-taking or loss of interest.
Funding rate — The interest rate that traders using leverage (borrowed money) pay to each other every 8 hours on perpetual contracts. Positive funding means long traders pay shorts (bullish pressure); negative funding means shorts pay longs (bearish pressure).
Liquidation — Forced closure of a leveraged position when losses exceed the trader's margin (collateral). During sharp price moves, liquidations can cascade, amplifying the decline.
Long and short — A long position means owning the asset or betting the price will rise. A short position means owing the asset or betting the price will fall. The long/short ratio compares how many traders hold long versus short positions.
Mark price — The official price used to calculate profit/loss and liquidation on leveraged positions. It is typically the time-weighted average of trades across multiple exchanges, designed to prevent manipulation.
Market breadth — The percentage of tradeable assets (coins or stocks) that rose on a given day. Breadth below 30% is often bearish (most assets falling), above 70% is bullish (most rising). 15% is extreme weakness.
Open interest — The total number of outstanding derivative contracts (futures, perpetuals, options) still open, measured in coins or US dollars. Rising open interest with falling prices can signal liquidation risk.
Open-interest change — The percentage shift in total open positions over a period (usually 24 hours). A decrease means positions are closing; an increase means new contracts are being opened.
Perpetual contract (perp) — A leveraged derivative that mimics owning the asset but never expires. Traders use it to amplify gains or hedge spot holdings without taking custody of coins.
Spot market — Buying and selling cryptocurrencies outright for immediate delivery, without leverage. Spot ETFs track this market and hold real Bitcoin or Ethereum.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of market-order buys to market-order sells. Above 1.0 means more buyers are being aggressive (pushing price up); below 1.0 means more sellers are aggressive (pushing price down).
Top-trader long/short ratio — The long/short positioning of the largest traders on an exchange, not all traders. A ratio of 1.6× means top traders are 60% more long than short, a sign of insider confidence or conviction.
