🗿 The day, explained simply

On Thursday, Bitcoin (the biggest cryptocurrency) went up 1.5% in value, reaching about $84,829. Ethereum, the second-biggest crypto, went up 0.76%. Most other cryptocurrencies went up too (about 53% of them). During the day, the US government released factory data that showed prices going up, which normally would worry investors in cryptocurrencies. But a big piece of good news came out: the SEC (the main US regulator for money) said it would make new rules that let investment companies hold Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies for their customers — something they couldn't clearly do before. Later in the day, a platform called NEAR Intents had a computer problem that let a hacker steal $3.8 million. The platform promised to give the money back to customers. Overall, it was a normal day with some good news and some bad news.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The difference between the spot price of an asset (what you pay right now) and its futures price (what you pay for future delivery). A positive basis means futures trade higher than spot.
Breadth — The percentage of cryptocurrencies in the market that traded higher versus lower on a given day; 53% breadth means more coins went up than down.
ETF flows (inflows/outflows) — The daily net buying or selling of shares in exchange-traded funds (ETFs). Positive flows (inflows) mean investors bought more shares than they sold; negative flows (outflows) mean the opposite.
Funding rate — A payment that traders holding long positions (betting price up) pay to traders holding short positions (betting price down), or vice versa. Positive rates encourage buying; negative rates encourage selling. Rates are typically charged or paid every 8 hours on major exchanges.
Long position — A bet that the price of an asset will go up.
Long/short ratio — The number of traders betting the price will go up (long) divided by the number betting it will go down (short). A ratio above 1.0 means more traders are betting on up; below 1.0 means more are betting on down.
Mark price — The official price used to calculate profits and losses on a perpetual futures contract, typically calculated as a weighted average of recent trades across exchanges to prevent manipulation.
Open interest — The total number of unclosed contracts (either long or short) in a futures market at a given moment. Growing open interest means more money is entering the market; shrinking open interest means traders are closing positions.
Open-interest change — The percentage increase or decrease in total open interest over a given period (usually one day). A 3% increase means $3 in new open interest arrived for every $100 that existed before.
Perpetual futures (perps) — A futures contract with no expiration date, designed to track the spot price of an asset (like Bitcoin) using funding rates to keep the two prices in sync. Traders use leverage (borrowing) to amplify gains or losses.
Short position — A bet that the price of an asset will go down.
Spot price — The actual price of an asset right now in the physical market, as opposed to futures prices (which are for future delivery).
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of aggressive buy orders (takers) to aggressive sell orders (takers) on an exchange. Above 1.0 means more buying pressure; below 1.0 means more selling pressure.
Top-position ratio — The long-to-short ratio among only the largest traders in the market, rather than all traders. Useful for tracking smart money positioning.
Volume — The total dollar value (or number of coins) traded in a given period; higher volume suggests more activity and typically higher confidence in price moves.
