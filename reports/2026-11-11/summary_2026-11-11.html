🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin is digital money, Ethereum is another kind. Today, the prices barely moved. But something important happened: people are taking money OUT of Bitcoin and Ethereum investment funds (ETFs), and stablecoins (special cryptocurrencies that are supposed to stay at $1) disappeared from the market—$14.2 billion worth in just one month, the most since 2022. That's bad because it means less money available to buy Bitcoin and make the price go up. But here's the strange part: the biggest traders (whales) are BUYING more Bitcoin while regular people are selling and taking losses. South Korea's computer chip companies did really well (Samsung +5.8%, SK Hynix +6.7%), which made traders feel a bit better about AI technology. The small token AGT jumped 205% because a big token release is coming soon. Overall, this was a worrying day because less money is available and people are pulling out.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

ETF (exchange-traded fund) — An investment fund that holds Bitcoin, Ethereum, or other assets and trades on a regular stock exchange. When investors buy an ETF, they own a share of the underlying asset without holding it directly. Inflows mean new money is buying; outflows mean money is being withdrawn.
Funding rate — A periodic payment between traders with long and short positions on perpetual futures contracts. Positive rates mean long traders pay shorts, encouraging shorts to open. Negative rates mean shorts pay longs, encouraging longs to open. Higher rates suggest the market is more confident about price increases.
Long-to-short ratio (long/short ratio) — The number of traders betting prices will go up (longs) divided by the number betting prices will go down (shorts). A ratio of 2.9 means 2.9 longs for every 1 short—a bullish market structure. Ratios above 2.0 are considered very bullish; below 1.0 is bearish.
Mark price — The reference price used for settling perpetual futures contracts. It is calculated to prevent extreme moves and is usually close to the spot price (the price on regular exchanges).
Market breadth — The percentage of assets in a market that are moving up versus down on a given day. A breadth of 33.6% up means about one-third of Binance's perpetual contracts rose, while two-thirds fell—a sign of broad selling.
Open interest — The total number of contracts (or coins' worth of contracts) still open and unsettled on a futures exchange. An increase suggests traders are opening new positions; a decrease suggests positions are being closed or liquidated.
Open-interest change (OI change) — The percentage shift in open interest over a period (usually 24 hours). A decline of 2.3% means the total amount of unsettled positions fell—traders are exiting or being forced out.
Stablecoin — A cryptocurrency designed to maintain a stable value, usually pegged to the US dollar at $1. Examples include USDT (Tether) and USDC (Circle). They are used as cash in crypto markets.
Taker buy/sell ratio — A measure of aggressive buying versus aggressive selling in the immediate price action. A ratio of 1.0 means equal aggressive buying and selling. Above 1.0 favors buyers; below 1.0 favors sellers. A ratio of 0.93 means sellers are slightly more aggressive.
Top position ratio — The long-to-short ratio among the largest traders on an exchange, not all accounts. Provides insight into what the biggest money is doing, separately from retail traders.
