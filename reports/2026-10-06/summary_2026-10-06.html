🗿 The day, explained simply

On October 6, Bitcoin and Ethereum both went down a little bit — Bitcoin fell from about $85,700 to $85,500, and Ethereum fell a bit more from $2,708 to $2,696. Most cryptocurrencies didn't do well that day; only about 36 out of every 100 coins went up in price. But some companies made big announcements: a major investment bank and other financial companies put money into the OKX crypto trading platform, and a company called Winklevoss (run by the twins who got Bitcoin early) filed paperwork to create a new investment fund for Zcash, a privacy coin. There was also concern when someone said the Tether stablecoin company froze their money without explanation. Overall it was a calm but discouraging day for most traders, with the decline driven by weak demand rather than bad news.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Annualized funding rate — The annual return (as a percentage) that traders pay or receive for holding leveraged positions on a perpetual futures contract. Positive rates mean long traders pay shorts; negative means shorts pay longs. Reflects market sentiment and leverage levels.
Breadth — The percentage of all coins trading up versus down on an exchange on a given day. High breadth (many coins up) signals broad bullish sentiment; low breadth (many coins down) signals weakness.
Funding rate — A small periodic payment between long and short traders on perpetual futures contracts, designed to keep the contract price close to the spot price. Paid every 8 hours on most exchanges.
Long/short ratio — The ratio of open contracts betting on price rises (longs) versus price falls (shorts). A ratio above 1.0 means more traders are long; below 1.0 means more are short.
Mark price — The exchange's calculated reference price for a perpetual futures contract, used to calculate gains/losses and to trigger liquidations. Different from the last traded price; designed to prevent manipulation.
Open interest — The total number of open long and short contracts (or the total dollar value of those contracts) on a perpetual futures exchange for a given coin pair.
Taker buy/sell ratio — The proportion of market-impact trades (those that fill existing orders) that were buys versus sells. A ratio above 1.0 means more aggressive buying; below 1.0 means more aggressive selling.
Top trader long/short ratio — The ratio of long to short positions held by the largest traders (whale accounts) on an exchange. Shows what the most influential traders are betting on.
