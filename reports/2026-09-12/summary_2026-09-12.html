07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

24-hour volume — How much of a coin changed hands in the last day, in US dollars. More volume means more activity and usually easier buying and selling.
Annualised (funding) — A fee that is actually charged every 8 hours, shown as if it ran for a whole year — so a rate on one exchange can be compared fairly with another.
Funding rate — A small fee swapped between the two sides of a perpetual every 8 hours. Positive means the traders betting on a rise pay those betting on a fall; negative is the reverse.
Mark price — A smoothed 'fair' price the exchange calculates, used to decide things like forced closures, so a brief spike in the live price doesn't unfairly close people out.
Open interest — The total amount of money sitting in open bets on a contract right now. Rising open interest means new money is coming in; falling means traders are closing out.
Perpetual — A futures contract with no expiry date — the most common way crypto is traded on these venues. It's kept in line with the real coin price by the funding fee.
S&P 500 / Nasdaq — The two main US stock-market indexes. Crypto often drifts in the same direction as these on broadly risk-on or risk-off days.
Treasuries / 10-year yield — US government bonds. When their price falls, the interest they pay (the yield) rises, which can pull money away from riskier assets like crypto.
US Dollar Index — A measure of the dollar's strength against other big currencies. A stronger dollar is often a mild headwind for crypto.
