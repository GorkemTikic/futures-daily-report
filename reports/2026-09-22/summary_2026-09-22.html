Bitcoin and Ethereum both went down a small amount on September 22—not even half a percent. 07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Altcoins — Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin or Ethereum. Altcoins typically have smaller market capitalizations and higher volatility, ranging from established projects (Solana, Cardano) to speculative tokens with low liquidity.
Basis — In the context of daily price changes, the time period over which the price change is measured. 'utc-day' means the change from market open to close within a single UTC day; 'rolling-24h' means the change over the most recent 24-hour period regardless of calendar dates.
ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) — A fund that holds Bitcoin or Ethereum on behalf of investors and trades on traditional stock exchanges like the NYSE. Spot Bitcoin ETFs hold actual Bitcoin; flows in and out of these funds reflect institutional investor demand for exposure without holding crypto directly.
Fear & Greed Index — A sentiment indicator (scored 0–100) that measures overall market psychology toward risk. Scores below 25 = 'Extreme Fear' (potential buying opportunity); 50 = neutral; above 75 = 'Extreme Greed' (potential sell-off risk). The index fell from 78 (Extreme Greed) to 71 (Greed) on September 22.
Funding rate — A periodic payment between traders on perpetual futures contracts to keep the contract price close to the spot price. Positive funding means long (bullish) traders pay short traders; negative means shorts pay longs. Higher funding signals more aggressive bullish positioning and trader confidence that prices will rise.
Liquidation — Forced closure of a leveraged position when the value of collateral (security deposit) falls below a required minimum. Liquidations happen automatically if price moves sharply against the trader, causing sudden market sales that can accelerate price declines.
Long-to-short ratio — The proportion of open bets that traders have placed betting price will go up (longs) compared to bets that price will go down (shorts). A ratio above 1.0 means more bullish positioning; below 1.0 means more bearish. Extreme ratios can signal crowded trades vulnerable to quick reversals.
Mark price — The official reference price used by exchanges to calculate profit and loss on perpetual futures contracts. Mark price differs from the last traded price to prevent manipulation and ensure fair liquidations, typically calculated as a weighted average of spot prices and funding.
Net flow (or net inflow/outflow) — The cumulative amount of money entering (inflow) or leaving (outflow) investment vehicles like ETFs over a specific period. Positive net flow suggests growing investor confidence; negative flow suggests selling pressure or reduced interest.
Open interest — The total amount of money locked into unfilled futures or derivatives contracts on an exchange at a given time. Rising open interest signals growing trader engagement and potential for larger price moves; falling open interest suggests traders are closing positions or reducing risk.
Open interest change — The percentage increase or decrease in open interest over a single day or reporting period. Positive change means new money is entering the market; negative means money is leaving. Changes correlate with shifts in trader conviction or risk appetite.
Perpetual futures (or perps) — Derivatives contracts that let traders bet on future price moves with leverage (borrowed money), with no expiration date. Unlike regular futures, perpetuals stay linked to the spot price via funding rates and are settled continuously.
Spot price — The current market price for immediate delivery of an asset (e.g., Bitcoin at $84,500). Spot prices are set by real-time supply and demand in cash markets, in contrast to futures prices which can differ.
