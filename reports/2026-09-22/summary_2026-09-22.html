The day in one line

On September 22, 2026, Bitcoin and Ethereum prices each fell about half a percent to $86,158 and $2,752 respectively, despite strong institutional buying pressure including a record Bitcoin ETF inflow day and a major Binance-Circle investment deal.

🦴 Caveman mode
🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin and Ethereum both went down about half a percent during the day—Bitcoin ended at about $86,158, Ethereum at about $2,752. The big news was that people who manage large amounts of money (called institutional investors) actually bought a lot: Bitcoin ETF funds absorbed their biggest single day of buying in two years, and the crypto exchange Binance bought a $100 million stake in Circle, a company that makes a popular stablecoin (a type of digital money). Also, the futures exchange CME announced it will start letting people trade Bitcoin Cash and Uniswap contracts, and Canada's biggest banks said they're working together to test moving money around using blockchain technology. The scary part wasn't about the crypto itself—it was that people seemed nervous about funding rates (the cost to borrow money for leveraged bets), which got extremely high on some exchanges, suggesting people were betting very aggressively.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The difference between a futures contract price and the spot price of the underlying asset. A positive basis means the futures contract trades above spot; negative basis means it trades below. Used to calculate potential arbitrage opportunities.
Cash-settled — A derivatives contract where profits and losses are paid in cash (usually dollars) at expiration, rather than delivering the physical underlying asset. The contract pays based on the difference between entry price and exit price.
Funding rate — The periodic payment exchanged between long and short traders on perpetual futures contracts to keep the contract price aligned with the spot price. High positive rates mean longs pay shorts; negative rates mean shorts pay longs. Annualized funding rate scales the periodic rate to an annual percentage.
Leverage — Using borrowed capital to increase the size of a trading position beyond what the trader's own money can support. A 10x leveraged position means controlling $10 for every $1 of capital risked. Amplifies both gains and losses.
Liquidation — The forced closure of a leveraged trading position when losses reduce the trader's collateral below the minimum required. The position is automatically sold at market price, often triggering additional losses.
Long/short ratio — The proportion of traders holding long positions (betting price will rise) versus short positions (betting price will fall). A ratio above 1.0 indicates more longs; below 1.0 indicates more shorts. Used to gauge trader sentiment.
Mark price — The reference price used on perpetual futures contracts, calculated as a weighted average of prices across multiple exchanges and designed to prevent artificial volatility from thinly-traded spot markets.
Open interest — The total number of open futures or perpetual contracts (measured in coins or dollars) that have not yet been closed. Rising open interest during a price move suggests conviction; falling open interest can signal position unwinding.
Open-interest change — The increase or decrease in the total number of open contracts over a period. Growth in OI during a rally suggests new longs entering; decline suggests position covering or liquidations.
Perpetual futures (perp) — A type of futures contract with no expiration date. Traders hold positions indefinitely, funding each other via funding rates rather than settling at a fixed date. Commonly offered on crypto exchanges.
Spot ETF — An exchange-traded fund that holds actual Bitcoin or Ethereum as collateral, allowing investors to gain exposure without custody risk. Spot ETFs differ from futures-based ETFs because they hold the physical asset.
Stablecoin — A cryptocurrency designed to maintain a fixed price, usually pegged to the US Dollar (1:1). Examples include USDC and USDT. Used as a settlement currency for trading and transfers.
Tokenization / Tokenized deposit — Converting traditional assets (such as bank deposits, stocks, or fiat currency) into blockchain-based digital tokens. A tokenized deposit is a traditional bank deposit recorded as a token on a blockchain, enabling faster settlement and programmable payments.
USDC — USD Coin, a stablecoin issued by Circle Internet Group. Each USDC token is backed by one US Dollar held in reserve, maintaining a 1:1 price peg.
Volume — The total notional value (in dollars) of all trades executed on an exchange or for a given asset over a time period. Higher volume indicates more trading activity and typically tighter bid-ask spreads.
