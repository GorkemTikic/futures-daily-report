07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis — The price difference between a futures contract and the underlying spot price. A positive basis (futures trading higher than spot) usually means traders expect prices to rise.
Breadth — The percentage of coins or assets that moved up versus down on the day. High breadth (like 74% up) means most of the market is rising, not just a few big coins.
Funding rate — A periodic payment between traders holding long and short positions on perpetual contracts (futures with no expiration). High positive funding means longs are paying shorts, signaling bullish betting; negative funding means bears are paying bulls.
Long/short ratio — The number of traders betting price will go up (long) compared to those betting it will go down (short). A ratio of 2.6 means 2.6 longs for every 1 short. Higher ratios are bullish; lower ratios are bearish.
Mark price — The reference price an exchange uses to calculate profit and loss on perpetual contracts. It is usually close to but not exactly the last traded price, designed to prevent manipulation.
Open interest — The total number of open (not closed) perpetual contracts or futures positions. High and rising open interest means more money is betting on future price moves; falling OI means traders are exiting positions.
Open-interest change — How much the total open interest grew or shrank over a period. Rising OI often signals confidence or a trend building; falling OI signals uncertainty or a reversal.
Taker buy/sell ratio — A measure of aggressive buying versus aggressive selling. Ratios above 1.0 mean buyers are more aggressive (bullish); below 1.0 means sellers are more aggressive (bearish). Values near 1.0 show balanced pressure.
Top-trader long/short ratio — The long/short balance among the largest traders (measured by position size), not all traders. It reveals what the biggest money is doing and often leads smaller traders.
Venue — A cryptocurrency exchange or trading platform (such as Binance, Bybit, OKX, etc.).
Volume — The total dollar value of crypto traded over the reporting period. Higher volume usually means more liquidity and potentially more reliable prices; lower volume can mean prices are easier to move.
