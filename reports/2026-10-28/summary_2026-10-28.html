The Federal Reserve is the group that controls how expensive it is to borrow money in the USA. They decided to keep it the same, but two people there wanted to make it more expensive—this was a surprise that made some traders nervous. 07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Annualized funding rate — The annual percentage yield of funding payments in perpetual futures, calculated by multiplying the 8-hourly funding rate by 365÷8 (or similar). Shows the cost of holding leveraged positions long (or earning yield on shorts) over a full year.
Basis — The reporting period for price and volume statistics. 'UTC-day' means the figures cover one calendar day from 00:00–23:59 UTC.
Breadth — The percentage of assets in a market that closed higher (or lower) on the day. 57% breadth means more than half of all tokens rose, indicating broad-based strength.
Fear & Greed Index — A sentiment gauge ranging from 0 (extreme fear) to 100 (extreme greed), based on volatility, momentum, social media activity, and market dominance. Used to gauge retail emotion and risk appetite.
Funding rate — Periodic payment between long and short traders in perpetual futures contracts. When funding is positive, longs pay shorts. High funding suggests strong bullish sentiment and risk of a sharp reversal.
Long-to-short ratio — The ratio of open long positions to short positions among all traders (account ratio) or among the largest traders (top position ratio). A ratio above 1 means more traders are betting on a price rise.
Mark price — The reference price used for liquidation and funding calculations on futures exchanges, typically derived from spot prices and recent trades. It prevents manipulation relative to actual tradable last prices.
Open interest — The total value of all open long and short positions in perpetual futures. An increase signals fresh capital flowing into leveraged trading; a decrease suggests positions are being closed.
Open-interest change — The percentage shift in total open interest over a period. A 1.6% rise means new capital entered leveraged positions (or existing positions grew in value).
Taker buy/sell ratio — The ratio of market-taker buy volume to sell volume. Above 1.0 means more aggressive buying (buyers hitting the ask); below 1.0 means more aggressive selling (sellers hitting the bid). A ratio of 0.96 shows slightly more aggressive selling.
