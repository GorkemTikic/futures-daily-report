🗿 The day, explained simply

Today the Federal Reserve (the US bank that controls money) published notes from a past meeting, and one official named Christopher Waller said they should raise interest rates by a huge amount in December to 'shock' people about inflation being too high. When the Fed raises interest rates, it makes borrowing more expensive, so people move their money to safer places like savings accounts and bonds instead of risky bets like Bitcoin. Bitcoin fell 2.9% and Ethereum fell 2.6% because of this news. Almost every other cryptocurrency fell too — only 26.6% of trading pairs went up. Big Bitcoin mining companies also sold over $23 million worth of Bitcoin they were holding so they could build artificial intelligence computers instead. It was definitely a worried and scary day for people who own crypto.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis points — One hundredth of a percentage point (0.01%). Interest rate changes are often described in basis points; a 50-basis-point increase means raising rates by 0.50%.
Breadth (market breadth) — The percentage of trading pairs or assets that rose during a time period versus the percentage that fell. High breadth (many gainers) suggests strong overall sentiment; low breadth (few gainers) suggests weakness or panic.
Funding rate — Interest that traders holding long positions pay to traders holding short positions (or vice versa) on perpetual futures. High funding rates incentivize shorts; low or negative rates incentivize longs. Rates reset every 8 hours.
Liquidation — Forced closing of a trader's position when their account margin runs out of collateral. During market crashes, cascading liquidations can accelerate price declines.
Long/short ratio — The comparison of traders holding long positions (betting price will rise) to those holding short positions (betting price will fall). A ratio above 1 means more longs; below 1 means more shorts.
Mark price — The reference price an exchange uses to calculate trader profit and loss in perpetual futures. Used to determine when a trader's account runs out of collateral and triggers liquidation.
Open interest — The total number of futures contracts or positions held across all traders at a given time. Rising open interest in a down move suggests new shorts are entering; falling suggests forced liquidations.
Perpetuals (perps) — Futures contracts with no expiration date, allowing traders to hold long or short positions indefinitely. Perpetuals are funded by ongoing interest (the funding rate) rather than a fixed contract expiration.
Spot market — The market where you buy or sell an asset for immediate delivery at the current price (as opposed to a futures market where you lock in a future price).
Taker buy/sell ratio — A measure of aggressive buying versus selling pressure. A ratio above 1 means more market buy orders (takers) hitting the bid; below 1 means more market sell orders (takers) hitting the ask.
Venue — A crypto exchange or trading platform where futures or spot trading takes place (e.g., Binance, Bybit, OKX, Bitget, Gate).
