01 · Key terms

Four words, explained simply

Every coin on Binance Futures has two prices at the same time. Here's why, with no jargon.

1. Last Price

The price of the most recent real trade — the live price flashing on screen. It can jump around when the market panics, because one big order can yank it for a moment.

2. Mark Price

A calmer, "fair" price Binance calculates by blending sources and smoothing out spikes — the "sensible" price.

Why it matters: Binance uses the mark price to decide liquidations, and by default to show the unrealized profit/loss on open positions. When you actually close a trade, your realized profit/loss is based on the real traded (last) price — and you can switch the unrealized display to last price too. Liquidation, however, always uses the mark price.
3. Divergence (the gap between the two prices)

Normally the two prices are almost identical. Divergence is how far apart they drift, as a percentage. 1% is notable; 30% is alarming.

Why it matters: because liquidation uses the mark price, a big gap means you can be liquidated even when the live price never looked that bad. This gap is the main thing this report watches.
4. Volatility

Simply how much a price bounced around. A calm coin might move 2% all day; a volatile one swings 50%. More volatility = bigger chances to win and lose.

The one-sentence takeaway

The price you trade at and the price that liquidates you are two different numbers — and a big gap between them is where people get hurt unexpectedly. That's what we flag.
