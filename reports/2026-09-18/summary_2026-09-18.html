The day in one line

Bitcoin rallied 5.9% to $80,880 after Japan's central bank raised rates to 1.25%, weakening the yen, while the US CFTC filed crypto market rules with the White House following Congress's rejection of the CLARITY Act.

🦴 Caveman mode
🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin's price jumped almost 6% in one day to about $80,880 because Japan's central bank made interest rates higher. Normally higher rates hurt crypto, but this time something unexpected happened—the yen currency got weaker, which made investors think crypto was a better place to put their money. So Bitcoin went up. At the same time, the US government agency that makes rules for futures trading (the CFTC) sent its plan for crypto rules to the White House, because Congress couldn't pass a big bill about crypto rules. Meanwhile, the stock market had a tiny down day, but most cryptocurrency traders were feeling good. It was a good day for Bitcoin because it survived higher interest rates, but that just reminds us that even small rate increases can still be risky for prices.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

All-time high (ATH) — The highest price an asset has ever reached; signals strong momentum and can attract new buyers.
Basis point — One hundredth of a percent (0.01%); used to measure small changes in interest rates or yields.
Carry trade — Borrowing money at a low interest rate to invest in an asset with higher expected returns; disrupted when rates rise sharply or the borrowed currency weakens.
Collateral — Assets pledged as security for a loan; if the loan is not repaid, the lender can claim the collateral.
Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) — The US federal agency that regulates futures, options, and swap markets, including derivatives tied to crypto assets.
Derivative — A financial contract whose value depends on the price of an underlying asset (like Bitcoin); includes futures, options, and perpetual swaps.
Fintech — Financial technology companies that use digital innovation to deliver banking and payment services; often at faster or lower cost than traditional banks.
Funding rate — A periodic payment between long and short traders on perpetual futures that keeps the contract price aligned with the spot price; positive rates mean longs pay shorts, indicating bullish sentiment.
Geopolitical risk — Uncertainty stemming from tensions between countries or regions; can drive demand for safe-haven assets like gold and affect energy prices.
Innovation exemption — A regulatory waiver allowing companies to pilot new products or services that don't yet fit existing rules; used by the SEC to permit tokenized asset trading.
Leverage — Borrowing to increase the size of a trade; trading with leverage amplifies both gains and losses.
Liquidation — Forced closure of a leveraged position when losses exceed the collateral posted, usually executed at an unfavorable price.
Long / Short — Long: betting that an asset's price will rise; short: betting that price will fall. The ratio compares how many traders hold long positions versus short positions.
Mark price — The reference price used to calculate funding payments and unrealized profits/losses on perpetual futures, designed to track the spot price closely.
MiCA — Markets in Crypto Assets Regulation, the EU's comprehensive framework for regulating crypto service providers and asset offerings; comes into full effect October 2023.
Open interest — The total value of all open positions (longs and shorts combined) in a futures market; rising open interest can signal growing leverage or strengthening bullish sentiment.
Open-interest change — The increase or decrease in total open positions over a time period; indicates whether traders are adding or reducing leveraged exposure.
Perpetual futures — Derivatives contracts with no expiration date that track the spot price of an asset, allowing leveraged trading 24/7; they settle payments periodically via the funding rate.
Regulation Clarity for Digital Assets Act (CLARITY Act) — A US Senate bill that would have assigned clear regulatory roles for crypto to the SEC, CFTC, and banking agencies; rejected by the Senate on September 16, 2026.
Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) — The US federal agency that regulates stock markets and securities; increasingly involved in setting rules for tokenized assets and crypto trading platforms.
Spot price — The current market price for immediate settlement of an asset, as opposed to the futures price for delivery in the future.
Tokenization / Tokenized assets — Representing real-world assets (stocks, bonds, currencies, commodities) as digital tokens on a blockchain, enabling 24/7 trading and settlement.
Treasury (US) — The US federal government department managing the country's finances, taxes, and foreign currency sanctions; includes OFAC, which enforces financial restrictions.
Yen carry — A carry trade involving borrowing Japanese yen at low rates to invest in higher-yielding assets; unwinding accelerates when the yen strengthens or rates rise.
