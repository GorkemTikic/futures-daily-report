The day in one line

Bitcoin broke through $86,000 on technical strength and short covering, while Ethereum rallied alongside corporate treasury purchases and ECB's launch of blockchain settlement infrastructure.

🦴 Caveman mode
🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin jumped about 7% to $86,000, its highest price in eight months. Ethereum rose about 5%. This happened because traders betting the price would go down were forced to buy to close losing positions, and big companies like Strategy and Bitmine bought hundreds of millions in Bitcoin and Ethereum. At the same time, European banks started using a new computer system to trade digital versions of bonds. The worry is that traders borrowed too much money to bet on continued gains—if the price reverses, it could hurt badly. Overall it was an exciting but risky day.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Annualized percentage — A rate expressed as if it applied for one full year. A funding rate of 0.0001 per 8-hour period, compounded 3 times daily, equals approximately 10.95% annualized.
Basis — The timeframe used to calculate a coin's daily or rolling price movement. 'UTC-day' means the period from 00:00 to 23:59 UTC. 'Rolling-24h' means the most recent 24-hour window at the time the data was fetched.
Funding rate — An interest rate that traders using leverage (borrowed money) pay to fund their positions. A positive funding rate means bullish traders pay bearish traders, indicating the market expects price to go higher. High funding rates (above 6% annualized) suggest excessive leverage and risk.
Liquidation — An automatic forced sale of a trader's position when borrowed funds can no longer cover losses. This happens at the price set by the exchange, often at a loss, and occurs instantly when the margin requirement is breached.
Long / Long position — A bet that the price will go up. A trader who is 'long' owns the coin and profits if price rises.
Long-to-short ratio — The proportion of traders betting price will go up (long) versus traders betting it will go down (short). A ratio of 1.1 means 10% more longs than shorts; a ratio of 0.94 means slightly more shorts than longs.
Mark price — The current trading price of a futures contract on an exchange, calculated from recent trades. Used to determine whether traders are profitable.
Open interest — The total number of currently open derivatives contracts on an exchange. Rising open interest with rising price suggests new bullish positions are being added; falling open interest suggests positions are closing.
Short / Short position — A bet that the price will go down. A trader who is 'short' profits if price falls and loses if it rises.
Venue / Exchange — A trading platform where crypto futures are bought and sold. In this report, the venues are Binance, OKX, Bybit, Bitget, and Gate.
Volume — The total dollar value of all trades executed during a period. High volume indicates active trading; low volume suggests thin liquidity.
