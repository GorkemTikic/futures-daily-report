🗿 The day, explained simply

Bitcoin and Ethereum both dropped about 2% on September 23 because the US economy looked stronger than everyone expected. When the US government's borrowing costs go up (the 10-year Treasury yield jumped to 5.1%), people move money away from Bitcoin to earn that higher interest. A big Bitcoin trading platform called BitMEX also shut down completely at 04:00 UTC that day—it had been running for 11 years. A lot of people who had borrowed money to bet on higher prices got forced to sell (we call this liquidation), which pushed prices down further. The overall feeling in the market stayed calm ('Greed'), but it was still a worrying day because of all the selling.

07 · Glossary

Every term used today, in plain words

Basis points — A unit equal to 1/100th of 1 percent. When the 10-year Treasury yield rose 18 basis points, it rose 0.18 percentage points (e.g., from 4.92% to 5.10%).
ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund) — A fund that trades on an exchange like a stock. Bitcoin and Ethereum spot ETFs track the price of those coins and let investors buy exposure without owning the actual coins.
ETF inflow / outflow — Inflow = net buying into an ETF; outflow = net selling. Positive inflow means more people bought than sold; negative outflow means more sold than bought.
Funding rate — Interest paid by traders betting on price increases to traders betting on price decreases, or vice versa. Positive funding means bulls are paying bears, signaling strong bullish sentiment. Paid every 8 hours on major exchanges.
Liquidation — Forced automatic sale of a leveraged position when the price moves against it and the margin runs out. Example: a trader who borrowed money to bet on Bitcoin rising gets forced to sell if the price falls too fast.
Long / long position — A bet that the price will go up. Opposite of short (a bet that price will go down).
Long-to-short ratio — The ratio of bullish (long) positions to bearish (short) positions. A ratio of 1.16:1 means 16% more bullish bets than bearish ones.
Mark price — The price used to calculate gains/losses and margin requirements on futures and perpetuals. It tracks the spot price closely to prevent manipulation.
Open interest — The total number of open (unsettled) contracts on an exchange. When open interest falls, it means positions are being closed; when it rises, new positions are being opened.
Open-interest change — The day's percentage gain or loss in total open positions. Negative change (e.g., −9.6%) means traders closed positions net; positive change means they opened them.
Perpetual (perp) / futures — Contracts that let traders bet on price movements without owning the underlying coin. Perpetuals never expire; futures expire on a set date. Both use leverage (borrowed money).
PMI (Purchasing Managers Index) — A survey-based measure of business activity in manufacturing and services. Readings above 50 signal expansion; 58.7 (services) is very strong and typically triggers fears of higher interest rates.
Treasury / 10-year Treasury — A loan you make to the US government; the 10-year version pays you interest for 10 years. The yield is the interest rate. When Treasury yields rise, it makes bonds attractive relative to riskier assets like Bitcoin.
Volume — The total dollar amount (or coin amount) traded in a given period. High volume = many trades; low volume = few trades. Helps measure how much activity an exchange or market had.
